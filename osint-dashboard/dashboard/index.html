Reading this data

  • Loss rates are Ukrainian defense ministry claims of Russian losses. They are unverified, biased upward, and cover one side only; read them as a trend, not a count.
  • Oryx and WarSpotting count only visually confirmed losses, so they undercount, and recent months fill in as footage surfaces. WarSpotting covers Russian losses only; the Oryx tank-age figures come from a mirror last updated in February 2023, when Oryx gave about 2 claimed losses per confirmed tank or armored vehicle. WarSpotting data is © WarSpotting; only aggregate counts are shown here.
  • Tactic reports are paraphrased from ISW, RUSI and news coverage. RUSI and ISW items are expert assessments that explain how and why tactics change; they place tactics on the timeline and never feed the loss counts. Many ISW items were read through news reports of its assessments, and are drawn as hollow markers.
  • On the timeline, a report about both sides appears in both sides' rows. A row steps back to an earlier stage only when a report is about a new sector, where the tactic is starting again. "Countered" is separate from how widely a tactic is used.
  • ISW and RUSI write from a Western perspective with significant Ukrainian access; Russian-side tactics are often described through Ukrainian reporting.
  • Territory comes from DeepStateMap, a Ukrainian volunteer project. Its map is close to ISW's but not identical, and some day-to-day changes are redrawing rather than fighting. The council map uses Ukraine's pre-2020 village and town council boundaries.
  • Trend fits and trend-change months are statistics on claimed numbers. A change can reflect what the ministry counts as much as the battlefield, and the adaptation-speed figures rest on a few dozen reports. Comparisons before and after a tactic report leave out RUSI reviews, because they describe the year before.
  • Unit names are recorded only where a source gives them, and attributions sometimes conflict.
